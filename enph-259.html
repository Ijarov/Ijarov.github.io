Closed-loop motor speed controller

In ENPH 259, a lab-based electronics course, I built a DC motor speed controller from discrete digital and analog parts, then tested and debugged it one stage at a time.

Course
ENPH 259, electronics lab
Dates
September to December 2025
Result
Speed tracked the setpoint linearly (R² = 0.9996) up to about 4200 RPM
Tools
Op-amps, BJTs, digital logic, oscilloscope, logic analyzer, multimeter

How it works

An encoder on the motor shaft produces pulses as it turns. A counter counts them, a latch holds each count steady, and an R-2R resistor ladder turns that number into a voltage proportional to the speed. An op-amp PI controller compares that measured speed with the setpoint and drives the motor through a BJT stage, which closes the loop.

Debugging stage by stage

When the controller misbehaved, I didn’t try to debug it as a whole. I worked out what each stage should output, then checked them one at a time with the oscilloscope and logic analyzer until the measurements and my predictions agreed. That approach found a bent pin on the latch and miswired counters in my own build.

The swapped part

The course ended with a debugging test: the instructors took our working circuits and secretly swapped a component for a look-alike with a different part number.

Problem
After the swap, the circuit no longer worked, and the replacement looked just like the original part.
Search
I traced the signal along the loop on the oscilloscope, comparing what each stage received with what it produced.
Found
The input to the R-2R ladder was correct, but its output was wrong. The ladder had been replaced with a part that looked the same but had a different part number.
